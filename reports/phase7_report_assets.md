## Data and methodology (Phases 0-2, 4)

Full writeup: `docs/01_data_description.md`, `docs/02_data_usage_and_feature_engineering.md`.

- **8 selected lines** (4, 116, 117, 179, 401, 474, 541, 607), all 2024,
  12,683,862 trip-stop rows after filtering. Selected in two passes: peak
  headway <=600s, then a full-year completeness scan that dropped two
  original candidates (lines 1 and 3, 44%/64% missing real-time data) -
  doc 1 §2.
- **Two label-definition bugs found and fixed** while building this
  (doc 2 §1.3): an unbounded `headway_obs` let pairing-corruption rows (up
  to -47 scheduled headways) count as "bunched," and a signed rather than
  absolute-value threshold mislabeled large overtakes the same way. Both
  fixed; every number in this document reflects the corrected definition.
- **Data completeness**: ~92% of rows have a matched real-time arrival
  (13.1% null `headway_obs`, propagating from ~8-10% per-row missingness);
  weather null rate matches this exactly (7.9%), confirming it is the same
  missingness, not a separate join failure (doc 1 §4.2).
- **Feature set**: 26 numeric + 6 boolean + 3 categorical inputs across 8
  groups (current state, trajectory, leader, position, context, stop
  attributes, disruption, weather) - full table in doc 2 §3.

## Hyperparameter tuning and PCA

Full writeup: `reports/tuning_results.md`. Figure:
`figures/fig9_tuned_pr_auc_vs_horizon.png`. Test PR-AUC, tuned vs. original
settings (selection on validation, test scored once):

| k | HGB orig | HGB tuned | Keras orig | Keras tuned | LogReg orig | LogReg tuned |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.898 | **0.958** | 0.917 | 0.932 | 0.586 | 0.859 |
| 2 | 0.860 | **0.905** | 0.855 | 0.862 | 0.563 | 0.785 |
| 3 | 0.814 | **0.856** | 0.786 | 0.815 | 0.539 | 0.721 |
| 5 | 0.696 | **0.762** | 0.682 | 0.709 | 0.493 | 0.612 |
| 8 | 0.623 | **0.652** | 0.562 | 0.579 | 0.425 | 0.488 |

- Tuned gradient boosting leads at every horizon; the earlier "GBM and
  Keras essentially tied" result was about untuned defaults.
- **PCA did not help logistic regression on its own** (validation PR-AUC
  0.47-0.52 vs. 0.519 without it; only matched at 100% variance kept), and
  regularisation strength (C) had no effect.
- **What did help was a missing feature**: `abs(headway_ratio)` and
  near-zero flags took logistic regression at k=3 from 0.539 to 0.707 (0.721
  with PCA on top). This corrects the earlier claim that logistic
  regression's weakness reflected how nonlinear the data is - most of the gap
  was this one representation.
- Caveats: hyperparameters chosen on a validation month with no cold
  weather; small searches (12 / 9 configurations, 800k-row subsample), both
  winners at the edge of their search space; k=3 configurations reused at
  other horizons; Phase 6 diagnostics were not re-run on the tuned model.

## Split strategy comparison (different ways of splitting)

Full writeup: `reports/split_comparison.md`. Same model (HGB) and horizon
(k=3) trained under three different train/val/test strategies:

| Split | Test PR-AUC (whole test set) | Test PR-AUC (Nov-Dec rows only) | Validation % snowing / ice risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Temporal (primary) | 0.8136 | 0.8136 | **0.00% / 0.00%** |
| Random (trip-level) | 0.7915 | 0.8207 | 3.88% / 3.84% |
| Weekly | 0.7876 | 0.7995 | 2.81% / 3.14% |

- The whole-test-set column is not like-for-like (temporal's test is Nov-Dec
  only, the others span the year); the **Nov-Dec-only column** is the
  leakage-controlled comparison.
- **Leakage effect is real but small**: random beats temporal by ~0.007
  PR-AUC (0.9% relative) on the same population - the direction leakage
  should push, but not large enough to change any conclusion.
- **All three land in a tight 0.79-0.82 band** - the headline result does
  not depend on which reasonable split is used.
- **The validation-weather gap is real and total**: the temporal split's
  validation set has literally zero snowing/ice rows. Random and weekly both
  fix this. But weekly's *test* set ends up only 0.93% snowing (vs. 4.2% for
  temporal), a mirror-image problem - so neither alternative replaces the
  temporal split as primary, which stays the only one testing genuine
  forward prediction.
- One horizon, one model, one seed per split: indicative, not exact.

## D. Diagnostics

Full writeup: `reports/phase6_diagnostics.md`. Primary model: HGB at k=3.

- **By line**: line 474 is a clear outlier (PR-AUC 0.628 vs. 0.78-0.92 for
  every other line) - not explained by its bunching rate.
- **H2 ablation** (the plan's "most interesting single result"): delay-only
  features (PR-AUC 0.819) beat both dwell-only (0.803) and the combined set
  (0.813). **H2 does not hold** at this feature resolution - reported as a
  genuine negative finding.
- **Robustness**: dropping leader features costs 4.3% PR-AUC (relative) -
  graceful degradation, not collapse, when a vehicle drops off the feed.
- **Transfer** (RQ4): trained on 4 lines, tested on the other 4 - no
  meaningful degradation (-0.39%, i.e. none). Positive result, bounded to
  this 8-line high-frequency-trunk-route regime.
- **Scalability**: 6.1ms single-row, 0.006ms/row batched - not a deployment
  bottleneck.
- **False negatives**: concentrated on line 474 again (convergent with the
  by-line finding), not concentrated at peak hours, and not near-zero
  probability (mean 0.20) - borderline calls, not blind spots.

## E. Deployment recommendation

**Frame: a holding trigger at designated control points, not a network-wide
automated control system.** The evidence in this project supports a
narrower claim than "deploy this model to control the network," and the
recommendation below is scoped to what was actually validated.

**What the model can offer, concretely**: the tuned gradient boosting
model (`reports/tuning_results.md`) reaches recall 0.96 at 80% precision at
k=1 (~1 minute lead time), 0.79 at k=3 (~3 minutes), 0.59 at k=5 (~5
minutes) and 0.34 at k=8 (~8 minutes). (The untuned model, which the first
version of this section quoted, managed 0.95 / 0.75 / 0.47 / 0.20 at
k = 1 / 3 / 5 / 8 - tuning moved these figures materially, which is worth
knowing when reading any earlier number.) Read operationally: **a 3-5 minute
warning window is where this model is actually usable** - at k=3-5 it
catches roughly 60-80% of upcoming bunching events at a 20% false-alarm
share of its alerts, enough time for a control-room operator to act (hold a
following bus, or flag a controller-attention stop) - but at ~8 minutes it
still misses two thirds of events at that precision, which is not enough for
fully automated, unattended triggering. This lines up with the plan's H3
expectation of usable performance up to roughly k=3-5.

**Where**: the spatial hotspot analysis (Fig 4, `reports/phase3_descriptive_analysis.md`)
and the diagnostics breakdown both point to specific, actionable locations
rather than a diffuse problem:
- The central-Stockholm stop cluster dominating Fig 4's hotspot list
  (Garnisonen, Banérgatan, Skanstull, Värtavägen, Eriksdal, Musikhögskolan,
  Stadion, Östra station - all on line 4's corridor) is the strongest
  candidate for a physical control point: high bunching rate, high volume
  (>39,000 observations each), and line 4 is simultaneously the line the
  model is *best* at predicting for in the by-line breakdown, i.e. this is
  where the recommendation is both most needed and best supported by model
  confidence.
- **Line 474 should be explicitly excluded from any automated triggering**
  in a first deployment. It is the diagnostics' clear outlier on both
  measures (worst per-line PR-AUC, most overrepresented in false
  negatives) - the model's confidence on this line cannot currently be
  trusted at the same threshold as the rest of the network.

**Benefit estimate, as an upper bound only**: the EWT table above gives
irregularity alone costs riders roughly 1.0-2.6 extra minutes of average
wait per line, on top of what the timetable already implies. This is the
benefit ceiling for a holding intervention **if bunching were eliminated
entirely** at the observed rate - a real holding-control policy would
recover some fraction of this, not all of it, and the fraction is not
estimated here. **This is not a validated benefit projection.** Passenger
response to holding (do riders actually experience shorter waits, or does
holding just shift the wait from one stop to another) was not simulated
and cannot be, without a demand model this project does not build. The EWT
number is decision support for prioritising *where* an intervention might
be worth piloting, not a business case for its expected return.

**What would be needed before this becomes an actual control policy**
(explicitly out of scope here, stated so it is not mistaken for a gap that
was overlooked): a demand model to translate EWT reduction into realised
passenger-minutes saved; an A/B or before/after pilot at the recommended
control points, since the model's PR-AUC was measured on historical data,
not on a system that is being actively controlled based on its own
predictions (a holding policy changes the very dynamics the model was
trained to predict); and a decision on the operational threshold (this
document uses the 80%-precision operating point as a reference, not a
prescription - the actual threshold is an operational choice about
acceptable false-alarm rate, discussed but not decided in doc 3 §4).
